# Glioma segmentation literature review

## Scope and comparison rule

This targeted review covers 19 primary papers on glioma segmentation, BRISC, boundary-aware objectives, class imbalance, small lesions, and diffuse disease. Scores are copied in the form used by each paper. BRISC is a 2D, single-mask, contrast-enhanced T1 JPEG task; BraTS is generally a 3D, four-modality task with whole-tumor (WT), tumor-core (TC), and enhancing-tumor (ET) labels. Their Dice or IoU values are therefore context, not direct leader-board comparisons with this project.

## Synthesis for this project

Three published patterns match the frozen v1 evidence.

First, **small target size is a strong and repeatable failure factor**. The small-tumor study reports Dice below 0.45 for its small strata, and the cross-tumor reporting study observes downsampling and satellite-lesion failures. In this project, the predeclared small group contains 32 glioma cases, 21 failures, and a mean Dice of 0.393; its failure-risk difference versus other sizes is +0.430 with a 95% group-bootstrap interval of +0.258 to +0.592.

Second, **glioma is not a uniform boundary problem**. Diffuse lower-grade disease can have weak gradients, but BRISC supplies single contrast-enhanced T1 JPEG slices without grade or sequence metadata beyond the filename. The v1 audit finds only 31 of 204 glioma references in the predeclared sharp group and does not find a statistically separated sharp-versus-diffuse failure-risk interval. A boundary loss remains a justified experiment, but the current evidence does not establish diffuse boundary appearance as the sole cause.

Third, **context and topology matter**. The 60 failed glioma predictions include complete wrong-location predictions, fragmentation, and disconnected regions, while the image/reference pairs remain aligned. Multi-scale models, cascades, TTA, component filtering, and boundary objectives all have published motivation. The literature also shows that post-processing and TTA can improve some cases and harm others. The project therefore keeps each intervention separate and requires explicit meningioma/pituitary non-inferiority.

## What this review does not establish

- It does not make BraTS Dice directly comparable with BRISC Dice.
- It does not show that edema or enhancement can be inferred from BRISC JPEG appearance.
- It does not convert literature gains into expected gains for this model.
- It does not validate clinical use, patient-independent generalization, or scanner robustness.
- It supports the registered experiments; it does not change their numeric gates after results were observed.
